CORE PRODUCT PURPOSE

The game and character system should give users:

- An external source of motivation.
- A rewarding alternative activity during appropriate cravings.
- Visible progress that is not based on weight or body appearance.
- A short activity capable of redirecting attention away from an impulsive cue.
- A reason to complete plans, record outcomes honestly, and maintain consistency.

Do not claim that the game “hijacks the same chemicals” as junk food or directly replaces dopamine. Describe it more accurately as a competing source of attention, stimulation, novelty, challenge, movement, and immediate feedback.

The game is a behavioral tool—not medical treatment, a guaranteed craving cure, or a replacement for nourishment or professional care.

COIN SYSTEM

Coins should reward healthy engagement with the HabitWise process, not food restriction.

Suggested rewards:

- Complete an ordinary primary plan: 10 coins.
- Complete the follow-up questions honestly: 5 coins.
- Successfully move past or redirect an ordinary craving: 10 bonus coins.
- Complete an appropriate backup intervention: 5 bonus coins.
- Complete a recommended Signal Shift session: up to 10 coins.
- Reach a consistency milestone: milestone bonus.
- Complete a reflection or review a weekly insight: small optional bonus.

Do not award coins simply for going without food.

Safety and nourishment routes must never be described as failures. Genuine-hunger, glucose-safety, eating-disorder, illness, pregnancy, and clinician-directed actions should preserve the user’s streak when completed appropriately.

Prevent reward exploitation:

- Give each reward event a unique identifier.
- Make coin awards idempotent so reopening a completed check-in cannot award coins twice.
- Cap game-derived coins per day.
- Cap repeat-session rewards.
- Do not encourage users to fabricate cravings or repeatedly launch check-ins to farm coins.
- Keep an auditable local coin ledger containing the reason, amount, timestamp, and related session ID.

STREAKS AND CONSISTENCY

Do not build a harsh streak system that punishes one difficult day.

Use two progress systems:

1. Momentum Streak
   - Advances when the user completes at least one eligible HabitWise plan that day.
   - Safety or nourishment plans count as responsible engagement and preserve the streak.
   - Only one streak day can be earned per calendar day.
   - Include an occasional grace day or streak shield.
   - Never remove coins because a streak ended.

2. Weekly Consistency
   - Calculated from completed eligible plans divided by eligible check-ins.
   - Do not count urgent safety exits against the user.
   - Do not display a percentage until at least three relevant check-ins exist.
   - Describe it as consistency—not obedience, perfection, or food control.

Possible milestone bonuses:

- 3-day momentum streak: 20 coins.
- 7-day momentum streak: 50 coins and a cosmetic.
- 14-day momentum streak: 100 coins and a rare cosmetic.
- 30-day momentum streak: a special outfit, animation, or character trail.

Reward returning after a broken streak. Do not use guilt, countdown pressure, threatening notifications, or “you lost everything” messaging.

THE FIRST GAME: SIGNAL SHIFT

Implement one original game for the first release. Do not build multiple games yet.

Signal Shift is a short three-lane action game featuring the user’s customized character.

Basic gameplay:

- The character automatically moves forward through an abstract, colorful environment.
- The player swipes left or right to change lanes.
- The player taps or swipes upward to jump over obstacles.
- The player collects glowing “Focus Sparks.”
- Matching consecutive sparks builds a combo.
- Running into an obstacle resets the combo but does not end the session.
- The session lasts a predetermined 3, 5, or 7 minutes.
- The game ends automatically when the selected time expires.
- The player receives a score, a brief celebration, and any eligible coins.
- The equipped character and cosmetics appear during gameplay.

The game should be stimulating without becoming aggressive, frustrating, or endless.

Do not include:

- Food images.
- Weight-loss imagery.
- Calories.
- Gambling mechanics.
- Energy systems that require payment.
- Public leaderboards.
- Social comparison.
- Endless sessions.
- Punitive lives.
- Pay-to-win items.
- Notifications designed to create compulsive re-engagement.

Collisions should reduce the combo rather than produce a harsh failure screen. The user should always finish the session and return to the craving plan.

GAME DIFFICULTY AND MODES

Provide adaptive but transparent difficulty:

- Standard mode.
- Calm mode with lower speed and fewer effects.
- Reduced-motion mode.
- One-handed mode.
- High-contrast mode.
- Sound-off mode.
- Haptics-off mode.
- Color-vision-friendly palettes.
- A pause and immediate-exit button.

Difficulty may gradually adapt to performance, but it must never manipulate the user into playing longer.

The session must end at the promised time.

GAME RECOMMENDATION LOGIC

Signal Shift must not appear in every plan.

The recommendation engine should treat it as one optional intervention selected only when the current answers suggest that fast, engaging attention redirection is likely to fit.

Situations where the game may be appropriate:

- Boredom or understimulation.
- ADHD-related stimulation seeking reported by the user.
- A cue-driven environmental craving.
- An automatic habit loop.
- Procrastination or task avoidance.
- Low-to-moderate emotional discomfort where activation may help.
- A sensory-seeking craving.
- A craving connected to repetitive thoughts that may benefit from a brief attention shift.
- A user whose previous game sessions have repeatedly reduced craving intensity.

Possible eligibility requirements:

- No clear physical hunger.
- No active glucose-warning route.
- No eating-disorder restriction or compensation concern.
- The user is in a safe place.
- The user is not driving or operating equipment.
- The user has not disabled game recommendations.
- The selected trigger is compatible with stimulation or attention redirection.
- The current time and health context do not make a stimulating game inappropriate.
- The daily game-reward limit has not been exceeded.

Do not automatically recommend the game solely because the user has ADHD or another diagnosis. Use the present answers, user preferences, reported patterns, and previous results.

EXAMPLE EXPLANATION

When recommended, the Plan page could say:

“Why Signal Shift may fit: You reported low physical hunger, boredom, and a strong need for stimulation. A short, engaging task may give your attention another immediate target while the food cue loses intensity. This is an optional strategy, not a guaranteed result.”

Always explain why the recommendation appeared.

PSYCHIATRIC AND PHYSICAL CONTEXT

Apply the game strategy carefully across the existing health contexts.

ADHD or attention support:

- Consider Signal Shift when the user reports boredom, understimulation, task avoidance, or a need for novelty.
- Keep sessions short and automatically timed.
- Help the user return to the original plan afterward.
- Do not imply that everyone with ADHD will respond the same way.
- Do not allow an intended five-minute intervention to become an unlimited gaming session.

Anxiety:

- Use Standard mode only for mild or moderate restlessness when the user prefers stimulation.
- Prefer Calm mode or grounding when the user is highly activated.
- Do not recommend fast gameplay during panic symptoms if it may increase arousal.
- Let the user disable motion, sound, and haptics.

Depression or low mood:

- The game may serve as a very small behavioral-activation step when the user wants it.
- Keep the objective achievable and nonjudgmental.
- Do not frame low scores as failure.
- Avoid demanding streak messaging.

Bipolar disorder or possible elevated mood:

- Avoid encouraging extended, highly stimulating, or late-night play.
- Do not recommend Signal Shift when the user reports unusually elevated energy, reduced need for sleep, escalating impulsivity, or difficulty stopping activities.
- Prefer a lower-stimulation plan and suggest using existing professional support when appropriate.
- Never make diagnostic claims.

Eating-disorder concerns:

- Never award coins specifically for refusing food.
- Never recommend the game to delay genuine nourishment.
- Eating-disorder safety mode must override all game and streak systems.
- Completing a nourishment or support plan should preserve progress.
- Avoid “winning against food,” calorie, weight, body, and punishment language.

Sensory needs or autism-related preferences:

- Allow predictable game patterns.
- Offer reduced motion, reduced sound, stable colors, and previewable effects.
- Avoid unexpected flashes or forced audio.
- Let the user choose whether stimulation is helpful.

Chronic pain or fatigue:

- Offer one-handed controls and lower-intensity mode.
- Do not require rapid repetitive movements.
- Allow immediate pause or exit.
- Prefer rest when gameplay would add strain.

Sleep conditions:

- Avoid recommending highly stimulating play near the user’s bedtime.
- Use Calm mode or a non-screen intervention instead.
- Do not encourage maintaining a streak by playing late at night.

OCD or compulsive tendencies:

- Avoid perfectionistic scoring, mandatory daily play, repeated checking, or pressure to complete flawless runs.
- Cap sessions and rewards.
- Do not use “just one more run” messaging.

Glucose-management needs:

- A glucose safety warning must completely override the game.
- Never use Signal Shift to distract from possible hypoglycemia or another glucose problem.
- Direct the user to their established care plan.

PERSONALIZATION AND LEARNING

HabitWise may learn whether Signal Shift is helpful for a specific user, but only from repeated local observations.

Track:

- Trigger category.
- Subtrigger.
- Craving intensity before and after the game.
- Session duration.
- Game mode.
- Score.
- Whether the user completed the session.
- Whether the craving returned.
- Whether the user reported that the game helped.
- Time of day.
- Relevant optional context.

Do not declare the game effective after one session.

Require at least three relevant sessions before displaying a tentative pattern and at least seven before describing a stronger personal pattern.

Example Insight:

“Signal Shift was followed by a lower craving rating in 5 of 7 understimulation-related check-ins. This is a pattern in your entries, not proof that the game caused the change.”

If the game repeatedly does not help—or increases distress—stop recommending it for that trigger unless the user manually re-enables it.
